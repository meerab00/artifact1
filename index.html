Guided Questions

Worked solutions to the questions in the assignment. Each answer shows the key mathematical reason, not just the final number.

1. Classify all finite Abelian groups of order 72

72 = 2³ · 3². The partitions of 3 are 3, 2+1, 1+1+1; the partitions of 2 are 2, 1+1. Therefore there are 3 × 2 = 6 groups up to isomorphism.

Invariant-factor forms: ℤ₇₂; ℤ₂ × ℤ₃₆; ℤ₃ × ℤ₂₄; ℤ₆ × ℤ₁₂; ℤ₂ × ℤ₂ × ℤ₁₈; ℤ₂ × ℤ₆ × ℤ₆.

2. Tabulate φ(n) for n ≤ 20

φ(n) counts the integers from 1 to n that are relatively prime to n. These are exactly the generators of ℤₙ.

3. How many even permutations are in S₅?

|S₅| = 5! = 120. For n ≥ 2, exactly half the permutations are even and half are odd. Hence |A₅| = 120/2 = 60.

4. Element orders in D₆ and D₈

In Dₙ, order(rᵏ) = n/gcd(n,k), while every reflection has order 2.

D₆ (12 elements): one element of order 1; seven of order 2; two of order 3; two of order 6.

D₈ (16 elements): one element of order 1; nine of order 2; two of order 4; four of order 8.

5. Why does ℤ₂ × ℤ₂ have no element of order 4?

Its elements are (0,0), (1,0), (0,1), (1,1). Adding any non-identity element to itself gives (0,0), so all three non-identity elements have order 2. In ℤ₄, the element 1 has order 4. Thus ℤ₂ × ℤ₂ is not cyclic, but ℤ₄ is cyclic.

6. Find an element of order 6 in S₅ and determine its parity

Take σ = (1 2 3)(4 5). Its disjoint cycle lengths are 3 and 2, so order(σ) = lcm(3,2) = 6. A 3-cycle is even and a transposition is odd; their product is odd.

One-line form: 2 3 1 5 4.
